OVERVIEW

Arcadia is a science startup experimenting with new approaches to research, company creation, and publishing. Anecdotal feedback around Arcadia's website, however, highlighted that the website content was ineffective at communicating these nuanced ideas. As a result, there was a need to understand the gap between the website goals and our visitors' experience.

APPROACH

Mixed methods research sprint

ROLE

Creative Lead (acting UX Research), Spring 2024

Fully executed research project end-to-end

Background

Arcadia is an evolutionary biology-focused research incubator. It's a company that differentiates itself through novel approaches to how science is conducted, including a unique funding model and a principled approach to scientific publishing. This particular combo has allowed Arcadia to break away from the conventional structure of biotech startups, functioning instead as a research institute, company creation studio, and self-publishing body all in one.

As a result, there's no direct comparison to Arcadia — which makes explaining what Arcadia does challenging. Furthermore, Arcadia has been actively evolving its strategy, making it even harder to determine what to tell an external audience.

We had received anecdotal feedback that users were uncertain what Arcadia did, even after visiting the website. The feedback, combined with my own experience with copy writing, led me to my hypothesis:

Our website copy does not clearly convey what activities Arcadia participates in ("What do we do? What's our business model?") or what's unique about Arcadia (e.g. "How is Arcadia differentiated? Why should someone support Arcadia in particular?")

Study Design

Our website goals tied in to our business goals of driving scientist interaction with our published research, and building brand recognition. Additionally, I believe this was an important stepping stone project to familiarize the organization with design tools and design thinking. Previously, the organization had struggled with flip flopping on brand and marketing decisions based on the mercurial opinions of one or two individuals. My hope was that the design process would anchor future decisions in user data. This would reduce churn in decision-making and allow us to look past personal biases.

Website Goals

Answer for our audience:

  • What does Arcadia do?
  • How is Arcadia unique?
  • Why are the ways Arcadia is unique important?

Organization Goals

  • Drive scientists to interact with our published research
  • Build brand recognition
  • Familiarize organization with the design process

Testing Approach

In order to gauge whether our website was meeting its goals, I put together a two week sprint plan. This low-lift, rapid study would also help demonstrate how these types of UX Research studies could be highly impactful without much overhead.

Scope:

  • In scope: information architecture, copy content, visuals, and layout
  • Out of scope: user journey to reach the website, and the external site where published research was hosted

Recruitment

Target Audiences (4 main categories):

  • Recruitment pool (Why should I work here?)
  • External scientists (Why should I be interested in this different way of doing science? Why should I interact with Arcadia's non-traditional publications?)
  • Internal employees (How should we describe Arcadia to others?)
  • VCs/Angel investors (Why should I invest in Arcadia and Arcadia spin outs?)

I recruited 3 participants from each category, for a total of 12 participants. Because we were looking for highly specialized users, I preferred to recruit from a professional network rather than through sites like UserTesting.com.

Methods

I used a mixed methods approach. Stakeholders were invited to sit in as a fly-on-the-wall during interviews (unseen to the interviewee).

Generative study: Contextual inquiry

  • 30 min moderated interview
  • Stimulus: current website
  • This strategy would identify pain points of both the current website and the overall experience of understanding Arcadia.

Evaluative study: Cloze test

  • Unmoderated worksheet
  • Stimulus: 3 paragraphs from website with 10-14 blanks
  • A Cloze test was an elegant way to quantitatively measure reading comprehension. While most of our target audience would have a high reading level, they may actually approach landing sites differently (less focused, skimming, etc.) A Cloze test gave us a better signal in context.

Analysis

I conducted thematic analysis on interview transcripts, taking an inductive approach to identifying overarching themes. Four main themes emerged from the analysis:

Thematic Analysis

  1. Balancing content perception
  2. Desire for examples
  3. Gaps in content about differentiators
  4. Visual appeal

Cloze Test Analysis

I also scored the Cloze test and found it below the industry-accepted level of comprehension, even when factoring in synonymously correct responses.

Outcomes

I shared an executive summary with leadership, as well as the process and findings in a presentation with a broader group of stakeholders.

Website impact

  • Copy rewritten across across 5 main info pages to improve readability and comprehension. More nuanced ideas reserved for pages deeper in the user journey, such as in blog posts.
  • 3 information architecture changes proposed to highlight where users could find the more concrete examples they were expecting to see.

Organizational impact

  • Familiarized leadership with the design process and demonstrated how to turn anecdotes into actionable data. This has brought more buy-in for other design projects.
  • Gave the entire organization has more clarity on how to describe and differentiate Arcadia to outsiders, and more understanding of our target audiences.
  • Data laid the foundation for the overall messaging and inbound marketing strategy for Arcadia. This work sparked larger conversations around how Arcadia wants to interact with the public through other channels like events, social media, and activations.

Next Steps

  • Conduct a second round of testing with updated copy
  • Incorporate insights into the broader messaging strategy for Arcadia
  • Build on top of this low-lift, fast-turnaround study by discussing how more resources could be beneficial to informing strategy

Reflections

This was a great project at the meta-level to start building the foundation of design at Arcadia. I learned a lot about the stakeholders I was working with throughout the process — what was most important to each of them, how their intuition guided them, how best to arm them with frameworks for bringing their vision to life.

Key learnings:

  • If I could do it again, I would involve stakeholders more in debriefing and analyzing interviews. Key stakeholders declined to listen in during interviews due to time constraints, so analysis could be a good opportunity for them to hear the voice of the user without the time constraint of scheduled interviews.
  • If I had more time, I would also do some card sorting to gather data around information architecture. A key insight was that people were leaving the website because they thought it would be easier to find the information they wanted elsewhere. In addition to what they were looking for (which was captured), I'd like to have more insight on where they would look for it.
  • The Cloze test was such an elegant comprehension test - I'm definitely adding this to my regular toolbox of UX research methods.
  • If I had more resources, I would employ more AI tools on thematic analysis for a more efficient workflow. This project had no budget, but this helps gain leadership buy-in for future UX project with more budget by showing how anecdotes can be studied and translated into impactful outcomes.
